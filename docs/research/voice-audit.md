## Section 2 — German B2B voice register

Sources: celonis.com/de, hetzner.com/de, mittwald.de, bosch.de.

**Quotes:**
1. "Entdecken Sie unsere Lösungen" (Celonis DE — Sie)
2. "Zur Plattform" (Celonis DE — no verb, just preposition + noun)
3. "Mehr erfahren" (Celonis DE — infinitive CTA, no "Sie")
4. "Jetzt anmelden" (Celonis DE — adverb + infinitive)
5. "Wenn Prozesse funktionieren, funktioniert KI" (Celonis DE — Wenn/dann implicit, parallel verb)
6. "Günstige Dedicated Server, Cloud & Hosting aus Deutschland" (Hetzner DE — noun phrase, no verb)
7. "Produkte im Überblick" (Hetzner DE — section heading, two nouns)
8. "Sei frei! Jede Technologie managed." (Mittwald — Du imperative, dev audience)
9. "Technik fürs Leben" (Bosch DE — three words, brand line)
10. "Entdecken Sie das gesamte Portfolio" (Bosch DE — Sie + verb)

**Dominant register:** Formal "Sie" is the safe default (Bosch, Celonis, Hetzner). "Du" only when the audience is unambiguously dev/startup (Mittwald). Mixing is fatal — pick one per locale and hold it.

**Sentence length:** Headlines run 3-7 words. Sub-heads 8-15. Bosch's "Technik fürs Leben" sets the floor at 3. Hetzner's "Günstige Dedicated Server, Cloud & Hosting aus Deutschland" sets the upper ceiling at 8 for a noun-phrase headline.

**Headline form:** Noun-phrase declarative dominates ("Eine Plattform, unbegrenzte Prozessintelligenz", "Produkte im Überblick"). Imperative is rarer and tied to CTAs ("Entdecken Sie...", "Jetzt anmelden"). Verb-first headlines (English-style "Build X") feel translated.

**What doesn't translate from English:**
- "Unlock the power of X" → "Entfesseln Sie die Macht von X" reads as bad copywriting-school translation. Native: "Mit X arbeiten" or just naming the thing.
- "Game-changer" → "Game-Changer" Anglicism in DE marketing decks; ban it.
- "Supercharge" → no clean German verb; "beschleunigen" is honest, "boosten" is hype.
- "AI-native" → "KI-nativ" doesn't exist outside marketing. Use "KI-gestützt" or just describe what it does.
- "Get started free" → "Kostenlos starten" works. "Jetzt kostenlos loslegen" is the warmer version.

## Section 3 — Turkish B2B voice register

Sources fetched: logo.com.tr, komtas.com. Gap: mikro.com.tr, hepsiburada kurumsal, trendyol kurumsal (403). Compensating with Logo + Komtaş, both heavy Turkish B2B players.

**Quotes:**
1. "Tüm operasyonlarınız tek bir platformda!" — logo.com.tr — formal "siz" implied via possessive, single exclamation (Turkish business sites permit one).
2. "Üretim, finans, satış ve tedarik zincirini uçtan uca yönetin." — logo.com.tr — verb-final imperative, four concrete nouns.
3. "ERP çözümlerini inceleyin" — logo.com.tr — CTA: verb in 2nd-plural imperative ("siz"), no "hemen" filler.
4. "İşletmenize uygun Logo çözümleri için sizi arayalım!" — logo.com.tr — "we'll call you" instead of "click here" — institutional warmth.
5. "Geleceğin Akıllı İşletmeleri için Veri ve Analitik Çözümler" — komtas.com — noun phrase, no verb, formal.

**Register notes:** Formal 2nd-plural ("siz" + -iniz/-yin endings) is the working default. Turkish B2B avoids "harika", "müthiş", "süper" — those are consumer/Instagram register. The Logo CTA "sizi arayalım" (we'll call you) is more institutional than the typical SaaS "Ücretsiz başla". For OpSolid TR: prefer "Görüşelim" or "Bizimle konuşun" over "Hemen başlayın".

**Avoid:** "Tersi değil", "aslında", "gerçekten", "tam anlamıyla" — all soften and stretch. The Komtaş line "Yaptığımız her işin özünde veri ve yapay zeka var" works because it's a flat assertion with no hedging.

## Section 4 — Anti-patterns (the AI-stink)

Five sites where the copy reads AI-generated. Three lines each, then diagnosis.

**Workato:**
- "Light up your AI with Workato Enterprise MCP"
- "The enterprise context, trust, and accuracy you need to make AI work. Built on the #1 iPaaS."
- "The next generation of agents are built on Enterprise MCP"
Diagnosis: stacked buzzwords ("enterprise", "MCP", "next generation"), self-rank claim ("#1 iPaaS"), zero concrete deliverable. Every sentence could describe any platform.

**VeriPark:**
- "Enabling Financial Institutions to Become AI-First Digital Leaders"
- "AI-Powered Customer Experience Suite"
- "Built for the AI-First Financial Institution"
Diagnosis: "AI-First" appears three times in four lines. "Digital Leaders" is uncountable. Reads like a prompt asked for "B2B fintech hero text".

**Tines (partial):**
- "The intelligent workflow platform"
- "The future of workflows is interconnected"
- "The intelligent workflow difference"
Diagnosis: "intelligent" twice, "future of X" once, no number, no integration name. The first line is fine; the section headings collapse into adjective-noun mush.

**Linear (partial):**
- "Purpose-built for planning and building products. Designed for the AI era."
- "Make product operations self-driving"
- "Issue tracking is dead"
Diagnosis: "AI era" + "self-driving" + "is dead" — three different cliché shelves in one page. The main headline saves them.

**Relay.app:**
- "The easiest way to automate with AI"
- "Create workflows for any use case"
- "Get inspired and see what others are automating"
Diagnosis: "easiest", "any use case", "get inspired" — every line is the safest possible version of itself. No risk, no specificity, no person behind the screen.

## Section 5 — OpSolid voice cheat-sheet

**Persona:** the operations engineer who happens to be the founder. Speaks to other operators. Does not call them "businesses" or "teams" — calls them what they are: a 40-person manufacturer, an agency, a logistics shop.

**Sentence length:** 60% short (3-8 words), 30% medium (9-15), 10% long for a specific reason (a list of systems, a case detail). Never two long sentences in a row.

**Verbs we use:** build, ship, wire, connect, automate, run, operate, fit, fix, replace, route, hand off, retire, cut, free up, watch, measure.

**Verbs we reject:** unlock, supercharge, revolutionize, transform, empower, leverage, harness, ignite, light up, elevate, accelerate (when not literal), embrace.

**Adjectives we reject:** cutting-edge, next-gen, game-changing, best-in-class, world-class, intelligent (as standalone modifier), seamless, robust, scalable (when not specified).

**Imperative vs declarative:** ~30% imperative (CTAs and section pivots), ~70% declarative (claims and descriptions). Avoid imperative in body — it nags.

**Numbers over adjectives:**
- Wrong: "Significant time savings"
- Right: "12 hours a week back to the operator"
- Wrong: "Wide integration support"
- Right: "ERP, CRM, warehouse, billing, messaging — connected once"

**German register:** Default to formal "Sie". Switch to "Du" only on a developer-targeted sub-page (rare). Never mix within a page. Avoid Anglicisms: "Workflow" yes, "Workflow ausrollen" no.

**Turkish register:** Default to formal "siz" with -iniz endings. CTAs prefer institutional "Görüşelim" / "Bizi arayın" / "Toplantı planlayın" over consumer "Hemen başla".

**CTA grammar (per locale):**

| Tone | EN | DE | TR |
|------|----|----|----|
| Primary | Talk to us | Sprechen wir | Görüşelim |
| Secondary | See how it fits | Wie es passt | Nasıl uyduğunu görün |
| Reading | Read the case | Den Fall lesen | Vakayı okuyun |

Avoid: "Get started free", "Start your journey", "Unlock your potential", "Jetzt durchstarten", "Hayalinizdeki sistemi kurun".

**Heading rule (enforced):** no period at end of any heading in any locale. Ever.

**The test:** read a line aloud. If a 45-year-old operations manager would say it in a meeting without smirking, it ships. If it sounds like a deck slide, rewrite.
